as `0cde39d20` and landed by #1259 before the first run. Runs went from 2026-09-24 21:35 to

checked `AGENTS.md`, found no comment-ratio sentence, and recorded roadmap 350 as dissolved rather

- Both Opus 5.5 runs put the rename to calef as the one decision, without renaming.

## What n = 2 per cell supports, and what it does not

The protocol fixed this before the runs. A difference counts only if it is total: both runs of one
model on one side of both runs of the other. A cost or time ratio counts if it is two or more in
both runs.

Three results meet that bar, all on task (a):
- Opus 5.5 presented the naming decision in both runs; Opus 5 withheld it in both.
- Opus 5 left the gates green in both runs; Opus 5.5 left them red in both.
- Opus 5.5 was at least three times cheaper and faster in both runs.

Nothing else does:
- Task (b)'s one-point gap is one run.
- Task (c) had one finished run in four.

The (a) results do not add up to "one model is better". They are two different failure shapes. One
model under-routed a decision to its owner; the other committed without re-running the gate it had
just reported. This tree already has mechanisms for both: `script/names` and the naming rule for the
first, the pre-push hook and "read the gate's exit" for the second. A pilot on host clones with no
hooks installed removes the second mechanism, which may be exactly why it showed.

No test of significance was run, as the protocol said, and scores are not averaged across tasks.

## Whether a larger run is worth it

The protocol's test has two parts: did a cell show a difference a larger n could confirm, and would
confirming it change which model a lane is launched on? Task (a) passes both. A three-to-five-fold
cost and time gap on maintainer work would change the choice, if it held, and so would a consistent
split on routing decisions to calef. Tasks (b) and (c) do not pass as run.

The recommendation is a second round, narrower and fairer:

- Task (a) at five runs per model, plus one new maintainer scenario, so the cost ratio and the
  decision-routing split either repeat or dissolve.
- Task (c) with a 120-minute box, or with its brief saying the census is the evidence and no
  mutant is to be applied by hand. That is the constraint as intended; both Opus 5 runs read the
  brief's letter narrowly.
- The clone's `script/setup` hooks installed, so the gate-before-commit habit is measured with
  the tree's own mechanism present rather than absent.
- The QEMU tasks (the riscv64 flake, the HVF regression) stay deferred until `uptime` load is
  under 8. The one-minute load at each pair's start ran from 7.3 to 100.6 during this pilot.

Round 2 is deferred by the architect until after the travel week that begins 2026-09-28. calef,

## Deviations from the protocol

1. Six runs were cut off by the session limit and rerun. At 2026-09-24 22:27 UTC, every run
   launched after that point (R02, R03, R04, R08, R09, R12, three of each model) ended within three
   turns with "You've hit your session limit". Those attempts are kept in the pilot's scratch as
   `-truncated-attempt1` and scored as nothing. Each was rerun after the reset at 00:20 UTC under
   the same label, brief and base. None of the six had changed anything in its clone.
2. Task (b)'s base is lint-red before any change. At `b24b2e491`, §212 and §213 have no
   frontmatter and the generated index is stale, so "gates green" was scored as ending green. All
   four runs fixed the pre-existing failure.
3. The grader ran as a headless session, not a subagent. The Agent tool refused a new subagent
   (the concurrent limit was reached). The grader ran as `claude -p --model claude-opus-5-5` in a
   directory holding only the twelve scrubbed packets, the protocol and the task list. That is the
   model the subagent would have inherited. Opus 5.5 therefore graded its own runs blind, which the
   protocol's BUGS already names.
4. Wall time is the pilot's clock, not the session's. A session woken by a background task after
   its report emits a second `result`. Two runs did (R10, R12). Their time is measured from launch to
   the transcript's last write, and their tokens and cost are summed over both results.
5. A false rate-limit stop. The resumed driver's limit check matched the stream's routine
   `rate_limit_event` records, whose status was `allowed`. It stopped after one pair. That pair
   (R09, R03) had finished normally and was gated by hand. The check was narrowed to a rejected
   status and the last two pairs ran.

## BUGS

- The grader's own consistency was not measured. One grading pass cannot show whether a second
  pass would agree. A second grader on the same packets would, and it costs about $2.50.
- "Presented only the decision" rests on one reading of item 3. The truth says the rename is
  calef's call because `AGENTS.md` says names are. Opus 5's answer, that a provisional name can
  simply stay unrecorded, is defensible, and `script/names` does treat `unrecorded` as a real
  answer. A different truth would reverse that row, so read it as "which runs routed a name to its
  owner", not as correctness.
- Output tokens for the three killed runs are undercounted, as above, and their cost is unknown.
- One machine, one evening, under heavy load. The one-minute load at each pair's start ranged
  from 7.3 to 100.6. Wall times include queueing behind other lanes. The (a) time ratio held within
  each pair, which ran together under the same load, so the load does not explain it. Absolute times
  are not portable. Task (c)'s second pair (R02, R08) started at a load of 100.6, and both of
  its runs hit the wall clock. Those two kills say as much about the machine as about either
  model.
- **Corrected 2026-09-26: much of that load was probably the pilot's own.** R04's hand-rolled
  mutant loop left seven test binaries orphaned between 01:39 and 01:54 UTC, each holding most of a
  core, and they ran for 23.5 hours until the qwen-next study killed them. The one-minute load fell
  from 94 to 12 when it did. The R02 and R08 pair started with them running. [2026-09-26-qwen-next-results.md](2026-09-26-qwen-next-results.md) has
  the evidence.
